Governance, Social and ESG Certifications

Sustainability is decided in the boardroom. Without clear responsibilities, controls and consistent incentives, ESG data remains a communication exercise, and certifications become a cost instead of evidence.

The numberup to −28% off the INAIL insurance premium for companies investing in prevention beyond legal requirements (OT23 reduction).

The G in ESG

I sat on the board of the Port of Thessaloniki: there I learned that a board does not ask for principles, it asks for data to decide on. ESG governance is exactly that: who decides, with what information, under which controls, and how sustainability enters strategy, risk management and management incentives.

Today governance also covers stakeholder dialogue and new topics: the use of artificial intelligence, data security, responsibility for the supply chain.

The S in ESG

The social pillar is measured like the others: health and safety, gender and pay equality, welfare, training, staff turnover, the ability to attract talent, workers' rights along the supply chain. Banks and clients ask for these KPIs, but they weigh above all on the accounts: fewer injuries and less turnover mean lower costs, and a company that attracts talent grows faster.

Here too, value is measured in euros. Companies that invest in prevention beyond legal requirements can obtain a reduction in their INAIL premium rate: from 5% for firms with over 200 workers up to 28% for those with up to 10. And gender equality certification gives a 1% exemption on social security contributions, up to 50,000 euros a year.

Certifications: which ones really matter

Gender equality, ISO management systems, B Corp, benefit corporations, supply-chain ratings: there are many options and they cost money. I help choose the ones the business needs, because a client, a bank or a tender requires them, and that return more than they cost: gender equality certification, for example, also brings extra points in public tenders. Then I build the processes that keep them sustainable over time, not just on audit day.

Who it is for

Boards and sustainability committees, family businesses structuring their governance, companies considering a certification or becoming a benefit corporation.
