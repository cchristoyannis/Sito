CSRD exemption and the new assurance standard: is it really damage?

Decree-Law 193/2026 brings forward the Omnibus thresholds for 2026-27, while the Commission consults on a single EU limited assurance standard.

While Europe hurries to adopt the new assurance standard for the CSRD, in Italy we are rushing out a bridge decree to limit the damage caused by transposing that same directive. Is it really damage?

The bridge decree: the new thresholds

Decree-Law 193/2026 brings forward the Omnibus thresholds for 2026 and 2027: only companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450m in revenue remain required to report.

Out of scope: measuring still pays

For companies outside the scope, the issue plays out on two levels. On one hand, banks and large clients will keep asking for data, so reporting remains necessary. On the other, reporting is still an underrated efficiency tool, because measuring shows you where to act and where not to. Reduced to a compliance exercise, whether regulatory or supply-chain, it delivers nothing; if you measure, report constructively and act, you create value.

Still in scope: the single assurance standard

For those still in scope, the Commission has opened a consultation, until 18 January 2027, on a single EU limited assurance standard based on ISSA 5000. It will cover ESRS, the Taxonomy and digital tagging, and could apply from financial year 2027 or 2028. With a single standard, auditors and companies will finally share the same yardstick.

The obligation can be postponed. Credibility cannot.

My take

The exemption moves the obligation, not the demand for data. Companies leaving the CSRD scope can use these two years to do better with less: a few indicators to bring to the table with banks, such as emissions reduction, energy intensity, headcount growth and the ability to attract talent. They must, however, be measured as if an auditor had to verify them, with traceable sources and clear accountability. The assurance standard under consultation already says what will be checked: it is worth reading as a checklist, not as someone else's obligation.
